# Affective Lability Scale — plain-language guide

**What it measures:** Affective instability (Mood)

**Who it's for:** Adults · **How it's given:** Self-report

**Length:** 54 items · **Scores from 0 to 4

## What this test is

Affective Lability Scale (Affective Lability Scale (expanded)) is a self-report questionnaire that measures **affective instability**. The instrument is established, but there are caveats worth knowing (version, scoring, or derived-score issues).

## How it's scored

**Method:** mean. ALS (Harvey et al. 1989; expanded 54-item and short 12/18-item variants exist). Items cluster into mood-shift subscales (anxiety, depression, anger, elation, and sometimes empathy). Score = per-subscale mean (0-4). Validation (Harvey et al. 1989): alphas .70-.85 across subscales in clinical samples. No diagnostic cutoffs — dimensional measure of affective instability.

### Subscales

- **Anxiety lability** — mean
- **Depression lability** — mean
- **Anger lability** — mean
- **Elation lability** — mean

## What the scores mean

There is **no official cutoff** — this instrument produces a continuous score that is interpreted relative to comparison groups rather than a pass/fail threshold.
