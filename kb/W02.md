# WHO-5 — plain-language guide

**What it measures:** Psychological well-being (Well-being)

**Who it's for:** Adults · **How it's given:** Self-report

**Length:** 5 items · **Scores from 0 to 25

## What this test is

WHO-5 (WHO-5 Well-Being Index) is a self-report questionnaire that measures **psychological well-being**. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

## How it's scored

**Method:** sum. Raw score = sum 0-25; multiply by 4 for percentage score 0-100 (both reported in literature). Validation (Topp et al. 2015 systematic review): alpha = .89-.94 across versions; WHO-5 correlates strongly with other well-being measures. Cutoffs: raw <=13 (percentage <=52) = poor well-being, screening indicated for depression; percentage 0-50 = poor well-being (Topp et al. 2015).

## What the scores mean

| Score range | Band | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 0–13 | **Poor well-being** | At/below 13 raw — depression screening indicated (Topp et al. 2015 cutoff) |
| 14–18 | **Suboptimal well-being** | Reduced well-being; monitor |
| 19–25 | **Good well-being** | Above screening threshold |
