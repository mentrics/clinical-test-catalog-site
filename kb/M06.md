# HADS-D — plain-language guide

**What it measures:** Depression (Mood)

**Who it's for:** Medical/clinical adults · **How it's given:** Self-report

**Length:** 7 items · **Scores from 0 to 21

## What this test is

HADS-D (Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale — Depression subscale) is a self-report questionnaire that measures **depression**. The instrument is established, but there are caveats worth knowing (version, scoring, or derived-score issues).

## What the questions look like

Each item is answered on one scale: 0-3 per item (4-point ordered response; wording varies per item), past week.

- I still enjoy the things I used to enjoy.

- I feel cheerful.
- I feel as if I am slowed down.

## How it's scored

**Method:** sum. Sum 7 items (0-3 each). Original validation (Zigmond & Snaith 1983): avoids somatic confounds, designed for medical outpatients. Cutoffs per Snaith & Zigmond: 0-7 normal, 8-10 mild, 11-14 moderate, 15-21 severe. HADS-D is the depression subscale of the 14-item HADS.

## What the scores mean

| Score range | Band | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 0–7 | **Normal** | No clinically relevant depression |
| 8–10 | **Mild** | Mild depressive symptoms; monitor |
| 11–14 | **Moderate** | Moderate depressive symptoms; consider intervention |
| 15–21 | **Severe** | Severe depressive symptoms; active treatment indicated |
