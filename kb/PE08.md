# PAI — plain-language guide

**What it measures:** Personality pathology (Personality)

**Who it's for:** Adults · **How it's given:** Self-report

**Length:** 344 items · **Scores from 0 to 344

## What this test is

PAI (Personality Assessment Inventory) is a self-report questionnaire that measures **personality pathology**. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

## How it's scored

**Method:** algorithm. PAI (344 items): 4 validity scales (Inconsistency, Infrequency, Negative Impression, Positive Impression), 11 clinical scales (4 items each domain... actually 8 items each): Somatic Concerns, Anxiety, Anxiety-Related Disorders, Depression, Mania, Paranoia, Schizophrenia, Borderline Features, Antisocial Features, Alcohol Problems, Drug Problems + 5 treatment-consideration scales + 2 interpersonal scales. Scores interpreted as linear T-scores (M = 50, SD = 10) against Morey's normative samples; T >=70 = clinically elevated. Validation (Morey 1991/2007): alphas .81-.89 clinical scales.

## What the scores mean

| Score range | Band | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 0–344 | **T-score interpretation** | Clinical scales interpreted at T >=70 (clinically elevated) per official PAI norm tables — raw sums insufficient; T-score conversion requires Morey's normative data. |
