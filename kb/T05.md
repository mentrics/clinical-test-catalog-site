# DSS-B — plain-language guide

**What it measures:** Dissociation (Trauma)

**Who it's for:** Adults · **How it's given:** Self-report

**Length:** 8 items · **Scores from 0 to 24

## What this test is

DSS-B (Brief Dissociative Symptoms Scale) is a self-report questionnaire that measures **dissociation**. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

## What the questions look like

Each item is answered on one scale: Not at all (0), A little bit (1), Quite a bit (2), A lot (3).

<details><summary>Example items (8 total)</summary>

- In the past 7 days, I have had moments when I felt as if I were watching myself from a distance.
- In the past 7 days, I have felt as if I were 'outside' of my body.
- In the past 7 days, I have had moments when things that were happening around me seemed unreal.
- In the past 7 days, I have felt as if I were in a fog or a daze.
- In the past 7 days, I have had moments when I could not remember something important that I needed to remember.

## How it's scored

**Method:** sum. Sum all 8 items. Derived from the 20-item Dissociative Symptoms Scale (DSS); assesses 4 facets (depersonalization/derealization, disruption of consciousness/memory, dissociative amnesia, disembodied experience) with 2 items each. Validation (Blevins, Weathers & Davis 2017; also Armour et al. 2017): alpha = .85-.88 across clinical and nonclinical samples. No official diagnostic cutoff — interpret as a continuous dissociation-severity signal.

## What the scores mean

There is **no official cutoff** — this instrument produces a continuous score that is interpreted relative to comparison groups rather than a pass/fail threshold.

## Usage & licensing

**Public domain** — US government work (VA National Center for PTSD); free to use with attribution.
