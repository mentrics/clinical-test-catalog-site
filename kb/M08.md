# EPDS — plain-language guide

**What it measures:** Depression (Mood)

**Who it's for:** Perinatal adults · **How it's given:** Self-report

**Length:** 10 items · **Scores from 0 to 30

## What this test is

EPDS (Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale) is a self-report questionnaire that measures **depression**. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

## What the questions look like

Each item is answered on one scale: 0-3 per item (anchors vary per item, e.g. 'Yes, most of the time' = 0/2/3), past 7 days.

<details><summary>Example items (10 total)</summary>

- I have been able to laugh and see the funny side of things.
- I have looked forward with enjoyment to things.
- I have blamed myself unnecessarily when things went wrong.
- I have been anxious or worried for no good reason.
- I have felt scared or panicky for no very good reason.
- … (5 more — see the full item list on the detail page)

</details>

## How it's scored

**Method:** sum. Sum all 10 items. Original validation (Cox et al. 1987): alpha = .87; cutoff >=13 recommended (sensitivity ~86%, specificity ~78% vs. clinical interview); >=10 often used in screening programs with lower false-negative tolerance for follow-up. Item 10 (self-harm thoughts) >0 warrants immediate clinical attention. Designed for perinatal use (depression screening in pregnancy and postpartum).

## What the scores mean

| Score range | Band | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 0–9 | **Below screening threshold** | Unlikely perinatal depression (routine re-screen advised) |
| 10–12 | **Borderline** | Repeat screen and clinical judgment; many programs trigger a follow-up conversation |
| 13–30 | **Positive screen** | Probable perinatal depression; clinical assessment indicated |

## Usage & licensing

**Free** — Freely available for clinical and research use; developed with UK NHS funding. The authors request notification of use in publications.
