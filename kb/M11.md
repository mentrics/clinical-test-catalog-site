# PANAS — plain-language guide

**What it measures:** Positive affect (Mood)

**Who it's for:** Adolescents/adults · **How it's given:** Self-report

**Length:** 20 items · **Scores from 10 to 50

## What this test is

PANAS (Positive and Negative Affect Schedule) is a self-report questionnaire that measures **positive affect**. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

## What the questions look like

Each item is answered on one scale: Very slightly or not at all (1), A little (2), Moderately (3), Quite a bit (4), Extremely (5).

## How it's scored

**Method:** sum. Two independent sums: PA (10 positive-emotion words) and NA (10 negative-emotion words), each 10-50 (or means 1-5). Do NOT create a total. Original validation (Watson, Clark & Tellegen 1988): alphas .86-.90 (PA) and .84-.87 (NA). No clinical cutoffs — interpret relative to sample norms; PA/NA correlate with extraversion/neuroticism respectively.

### Subscales

- **Positive Affect** — items 1, 3, 5, 9, 10, 12, 14, 16, 17, 19
- **Negative Affect** — items 2, 4, 6, 7, 8, 11, 13, 15, 18, 20

## What the scores mean

There is **no official cutoff** — this instrument produces a continuous score that is interpreted relative to comparison groups rather than a pass/fail threshold.

## Usage & licensing

**Free** — Widely used for research with attribution (Watson, Clark & Tellegen).
