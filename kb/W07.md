# BRS — plain-language guide

**What it measures:** Resilience (Well-being)

**Who it's for:** Adults · **How it's given:** Self-report

**Length:** 6 items · **Scores from 1 to 5

## What this test is

BRS (Brief Resilience Scale) is a self-report questionnaire that measures **resilience**. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

## How it's scored

**Method:** mean. BRS measures the ability to bounce back from stress — the ONLY commonly used 'resilience' scale that measures recovery rather than protective traits. Score = mean of 6 items (6 of the original 11 items are fillers... verify against Smith et al. 2008 Table 1; the scored set is 6 items with 3 reverse-keyed). Validation (Smith et al. 2008): alpha = .84-.87 across 4 samples; test-retest r = .69. Interpretation bands (Smith et al. 2008): <2.90 low, 2.90-4.30 normal, >4.30 high.

## What the scores mean

| Score range | Band | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2.89 | **Low resilience** | Below typical range — difficulty bouncing back (Smith et al. 2008 bands) |
| 2.9–4.3 | **Normal resilience** | Typical range |
| 4.31–5 | **High resilience** | Above typical range — strong recovery capacity |
