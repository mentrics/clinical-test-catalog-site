# RSES — plain-language guide

**What it measures:** Self-esteem (Well-being)

**Who it's for:** Adolescents/adults · **How it's given:** Self-report

**Length:** 10 items · **Scores from 10 to 40

## What this test is

RSES (Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale) is a self-report questionnaire that measures **self-esteem**. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

## How it's scored

**Method:** sum. Sum all 10 items (5 reverse-keyed: items 2, 4, 6, 7, 9 — 0/1/2/3 scoring after reversing, giving 0-30, or 1/2/3/4 giving 10-40; both conventions published). Validation (Rosenberg 1965; Gray-Little et al. 2009 update): alpha = .77-.88; RSES is the most widely used self-esteem measure. Interpretation: 0-15 low, 15-25 normal, 25-30 high (informal bands, not officially standardized).

## What the scores mean

| Score range | Band | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 0–14 | **Low self-esteem (informal)** | Below typical range (heuristic band, not official) |
| 15–25 | **Normal self-esteem (informal)** | Typical range (heuristic) |
| 26–40 | **High self-esteem (informal)** | Above typical range (heuristic) |
