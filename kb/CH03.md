# SDQ — plain-language guide

**What it measures:** Strengths/behavior (Child/Adolescent)

**Who it's for:** Children/adolescents · **How it's given:** Self/informant

**Length:** 25 items · **Scores from 0 to 40

## What this test is

SDQ (Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire (self-report)) is a self/informant questionnaire that measures **strengths/behavior**. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

## How it's scored

**Method:** sum. 25 items: 5 subscales of 5 items (emotional, conduct, hyperactivity, peer problems, prosocial). Four problem scales sum to a Total Difficulties score 0-40 (prosocial is separate, strengths-referenced). Reverse-keyed: 10 items (items 7, 11, 14, 21, 25 always true/prosocial; plus per-scale keys per official sheet). Banding (self-report 11-16y): Total Difficulties normal 0-15 / slightly elevated 16-19 / high 20-24 / very high 25-40 (per UK norm data, sdqinfo.org).

### Subscales

- **Emotional symptoms** — items 3, 8, 13, 16, 24
- **Conduct problems** — items 5, 7, 12, 18, 22
- **Hyperactivity/inattention** — items 2, 10, 15, 21, 25
- **Peer relationship problems** — items 6, 11, 14, 19, 23
- **Prosocial behaviour** — items 1, 4, 9, 17, 20

## What the scores mean

| Score range | Band | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 0–15 | **Normal** | Total difficulties within normal range (self-report, 11-16y) |
| 16–19 | **Slightly elevated** | Borderline range; monitor |
| 20–40 | **High/very high** | Clinically elevated difficulties; assessment indicated |
