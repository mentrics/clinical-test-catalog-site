# ISI — plain-language guide

**What it measures:** Insomnia (Sleep)

**Who it's for:** Adults · **How it's given:** Self-report

**Length:** 7 items · **Scores from 0 to 28

## What this test is

ISI (Insomnia Severity Index) is a self-report questionnaire that measures **insomnia**. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

## What the questions look like

Each item is answered on one scale: 0-4 per item (varies by item: none/very mild → very severe; very satisfied → very dissatisfied; not at all → very much), past 2 weeks.

<details><summary>Example items (7 total)</summary>

- How severe was your difficulty falling asleep?
- How severe was your difficulty staying asleep?
- How severe was your problem waking up too early?
- How satisfied/dissatisfied were you with your current sleep pattern?
- How much did your sleep problem interfere with your daily functioning (e.g., daytime fatigue, mood, ability to function at work/daily chores)?
- … (2 more — see the full item list on the detail page)

</details>

## How it's scored

**Method:** sum. Sum all 7 items. Original validation (Morin 2001 / Bastien 2001): alpha = .84, test-retest r = .83. Cutoffs from Bastien et al. (2001) validation against polysomnography and clinical judgment.

## What the scores mean

| Score range | Band | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 0–7 | **No clinically significant insomnia** | Absence of insomnia |
| 8–14 | **Subthreshold insomnia** | Subthreshold; monitor, sleep hygiene advice |
| 15–21 | **Moderate clinical insomnia** | Moderate insomnia; treatment recommended (e.g., CBT-I) |
| 22–28 | **Severe clinical insomnia** | Severe insomnia; active treatment indicated |
