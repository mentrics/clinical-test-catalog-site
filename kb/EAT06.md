# ORTO-15 — plain-language guide

**What it measures:** Orthorexia screening (Eating)

**Who it's for:** Adults · **How it's given:** Self-report

**Length:** 15 items · **Scores from 15 to 60

## What this test is

ORTO-15 (ORTO-15 (Orthorexia Risk Questionnaire)) is a self-report questionnaire that measures **orthorexia screening**. The instrument is established, but there are caveats worth knowing (version, scoring, or derived-score issues).

## How it's scored

**Method:** sum. ORTO-15: 15 items (1-4 points each; about half reverse-keyed per the original Italian key). Sum all 15 (HIGHER = LESS orthorectic tendency — lower scores indicate greater orthorexia risk in the original scoring). Validation (Donini et al. 2005): cutoff <40 suggests orthorexia-risk tendency (sensitivity/specificity balance modest); ORTO-15's psychometrics have been questioned (Misra et al. 2019 critique) — several revised variants (ORTO-R, ORTO-11) exist.

## What the scores mean

| Score range | Band | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 15–39 | **Orthorexia-risk tendency** | <40 — possible orthorectic tendency (Donini et al. 2005; psychometric quality disputed) |
| 40–60 | **No orthorexia-risk indicated** | At/above 40 |
