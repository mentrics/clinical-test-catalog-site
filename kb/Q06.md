# SDS — plain-language guide

**What it measures:** Social/occupational impairment (Functioning)

**Who it's for:** Adults · **How it's given:** Self-report

**Length:** 3 items · **Scores from 0 to 30

## What this test is

SDS (Sheehan Disability Scale (short)) is a self-report questionnaire that measures **social/occupational impairment**. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

## Item text

The exact question wording is **not reproduced here** (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

## How it's scored

**Method:** sum. SDS: sum of 3 items (Work/school, Social life, Family life) or mean (0-10). Some versions include an item for days lost from work/school (tracked separately). Validation (Leon et al. 1997): discriminates treatment-seeking from non-treatment-seeking samples; widely used as a functional-impairment anchor in clinical trials. No formal diagnostic cutoffs.

### Subscales

- **Work/school impairment** — single item 0-10
- **Social life impairment** — single item 0-10
- **Family life/home impairment** — single item 0-10

## What the scores mean

There is **no official cutoff** — this instrument produces a continuous score that is interpreted relative to comparison groups rather than a pass/fail threshold.

## Usage & licensing

**Free** — Freely available for clinical/research use (Leon et al. 1997); see Q02 for the 3-item full version.
