# PROMIS Pain Interference — plain-language guide

**What it measures:** Pain interference (Physical Health)

**Who it's for:** Adults · **How it's given:** Self-report

**Length:** Variable items · **Scores from 0 to 100

## What this test is

PROMIS Pain Interference (PROMIS Pain Interference (Short Form)) is a self-report questionnaire that measures **pain interference**. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

## How it's scored

**Method:** algorithm. PROMIS Pain Interference uses IRT-based T-score scoring (item responses mapped to theta via healthmeasures.net lookup tables), standardized to T-metric (M = 50, SD = 10, US general population). Higher T = more pain interference with functioning. Multiple short forms (4a, 6a, 8a, etc.) exist — confirm which form the catalog carries. Validation (Amtmann et al. 2010): alpha ~.97. Cutoffs: T 50 = population mean; T >=60 commonly flagged as clinically elevated.

## What the scores mean

| Score range | Band | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 0–59 | **Within normal range (T<60)** | Pain interference at/below 1 SD above the population mean |
| 60–69 | **Elevated (T 60-69)** | 1-2 SD above mean — clinically notable pain interference |
| 70–100 | **Very elevated (T>=70)** | 2+ SD above mean — severe pain interference |
