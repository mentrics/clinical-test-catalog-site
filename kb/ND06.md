# BAARS-IV — plain-language guide

**What it measures:** Adult ADHD symptoms (Neurodevelopment)

**Who it's for:** Adults · **How it's given:** Self-report

**Length:** Variable items · **Scores from 0 to 54

## What this test is

BAARS-IV (Brown Attention-Deficit Disorder Rating Scale — Adult) is a self-report questionnaire that measures **adult adhd symptoms**. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

## How it's scored

**Method:** algorithm. BAARS-IV (18 items + 2 supplementary items): total = sum of 18 items (0-54). 5 domain subscales (Activating/focusing, Sustaining attention, Regulating alertness, Managing frustration, Working memory) per the Brown executive-function model. Total + subscales converted to T-scores against age/gender norms (M = 50, SD = 10); T >=65 = clinically elevated. Validation (Brown 1996): alphas .87-.94 across forms.

### Subscales

- **Activating and organizing tasks** — sum → T-score
- **Sustaining attention** — sum → T-score
- **Regulating alertness/sustaining effort** — sum → T-score
- **Managing frustration/modulating emotion** — sum → T-score
- **Working memory** — sum → T-score

## What the scores mean

| Score range | Band | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 0–54 | **T-score interpretation** | Total + subscales interpreted as T-scores; T >=65 = clinically elevated ADHD symptoms. Official BAARS-IV norm tables (age/gender strata) required for conversion. |
