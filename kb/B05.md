# DASS-21 — plain-language guide

**What it measures:** Stress symptoms (Stress/Burnout)

**Who it's for:** Adults · **How it's given:** Self-report

**Length:** 21 items · **Scores from 0 to 63

## What this test is

DASS-21 (Depression Anxiety Stress Scales-21) is a self-report questionnaire that measures **stress symptoms**. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

## How it's scored

**Method:** sum. DASS-21: 3 subscales of 7 items each (Depression, Anxiety, Stress). Subscale sums are DOUBLED (x2) in the standard convention to align with DASS-42 norms (each subscale 0-42; total 0-126 or reported as 3 subscales). Validation (Lovibond & Lovibond 1995; Antony et al. 1998): alphas Depression .94, Anxiety .87, Stress .91. Cutoffs (per subscale, doubled; Lovibond & Lovibond 1995 manual + Antony 1998): Depression normal 0-9, mild 10-13, moderate 14-20, severe 21-27, extremely severe 28+; Anxiety normal 0-7, mild 8-9, moderate 10-14, severe 15-19, extremely severe 20+; Stress normal 0-14, mild 15-18, moderate 19-25, severe 26-33, extremely severe 34+. Coverage fixed 2026-09-09: DASS-21 has NO meaningful single total — single band pointing to per-subscale tables.

### Subscales

- **Depression** — sum x2
- **Anxiety** — sum x2
- **Stress** — sum x2

## What the scores mean

| Score range | Band | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 0–63 | **Per-subscale bands (see note)** | Raw item-sum 0-63; multiply each subscale sum x2 (0-42) and band per Lovibond & Lovibond 1995 manual Table 3: Depression 0-9/10-13/14-20/21-27/28+; Anxiety 0-7/8-9/10-14/15-19/20+; Stress 0-14/15-18/19-25/26-33/34+. |
