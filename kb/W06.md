# LOT-R — plain-language guide

**What it measures:** Optimism (Well-being)

**Who it's for:** Adults · **How it's given:** Self-report

**Length:** 10 items · **Scores from 0 to 30

## What this test is

LOT-R (Life Orientation Test-Revised) is a self-report questionnaire that measures **optimism**. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

## Item text

The exact question wording is **not reproduced here** (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

## How it's scored

**Method:** sum. LOT-R: 10 items, of which 6 score (3 optimism + 3 pessimism reverse-keyed) and 4 are fillers (not scored). Sum the 6 scored items 0-24 (range note: catalog says 0-30 which is wrong — 6 scored items x 0-4 = 0-24). Validation (Scheier et al. 1994): alpha = .78, test-retest r = .79 (12 months). Higher = more dispositional optimism. No clinical cutoffs.

## What the scores mean

There is **no official cutoff** — this instrument produces a continuous score that is interpreted relative to comparison groups rather than a pass/fail threshold.

## Usage & licensing

**Free** — Freely available for research use with attribution (Scheier, Carver & Bridges 1994).
