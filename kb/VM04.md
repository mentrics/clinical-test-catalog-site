# VQ — plain-language guide

**What it measures:** Valued living (Values/Motivation)

**Who it's for:** Adults · **How it's given:** Self-report

**Length:** 10 items · **Scores from 1 to 7

## What this test is

VQ (Valuing Questionnaire) is a self-report questionnaire that measures **valued living**. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

## Item text

The exact question wording is **not reproduced here** (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

## How it's scored

**Method:** mean. VQ: 10 items, 2 subscales of 5 each — Progress (acting in accordance with one's values) and Obstruction (being blocked from values-consistent living). Score = mean per subscale (no total standard). Validation (Smout, Davies, Burns & Christie 2014): alphas Progress .88, Obstruction .85. ACT-based values-clarity measure (complementary to AAQ-II's flexibility measure). No clinical cutoffs.

### Subscales

- **Progress** — mean
- **Obstruction** — mean

## What the scores mean

There is **no official cutoff** — this instrument produces a continuous score that is interpreted relative to comparison groups rather than a pass/fail threshold.

## Usage & licensing

**Free** — Freely available for research use with attribution (Smout et al. 2014).
