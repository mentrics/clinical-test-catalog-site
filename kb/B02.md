# CBI — plain-language guide

**What it measures:** Burnout (Stress/Burnout)

**Who it's for:** Adults · **How it's given:** Self-report

**Length:** 19 items · **Scores from 0 to 100

## What this test is

CBI (Copenhagen Burnout Inventory) is a self-report questionnaire that measures **burnout**. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

## What the questions look like

Each item is answered on one scale: 0-100 scale per item: Never (0) / Rarely (25) / Sometimes (50) / Often (75) / Always (100); some items use degree anchors mapped to 0/25/50/75/100.

<details><summary>Example items (19 total)</summary>

- How often do you feel tired?
- How often are you physically exhausted?
- How often are you emotionally exhausted?
- How often do you think: 'I can't carry on'?
- How often do you feel worn out at the end of the working day?
- … (14 more — see the full item list on the detail page)

</details>

## How it's scored

**Method:** mean. Compute each subscale as the MEAN of its items, then rescale each item to 0-100. Do NOT sum into one total by default: personal (6 items), work (7 items), client (6 items) are reported separately. The CBI itself does not publish an official total-burnout score — 'Total Burnout' (as reported by Rilev) is a derived average of subscales, not an original CBI scale (audit flag). Original validation (Kristensen et al. 2005): alphas .79-.90 across the three scales. Cut-off guidance: ~50+ considered elevated burnout in Danish validation work; no official diagnostic cutoffs.

### Subscales

- **Personal Burnout** — items 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6
- **Work Burnout** — items 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13
- **Client Burnout** — items 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19

## What the scores mean

| Score range | Band | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 0–49 | **Low burnout risk** | Below commonly used elevated threshold (informal, per validation literature) |
| 50–74 | **Elevated burnout** | At/above ~50 informal threshold; examine which subscale drives it |
| 75–100 | **High burnout** | Severe burnout symptoms; intervention warranted |
