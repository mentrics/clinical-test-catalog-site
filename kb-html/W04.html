MLQ — plain-language guide

What it measures: Meaning in life (Well-being)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 10 items · **Scores from 1 to 7

What this test is

MLQ (Meaning in Life Questionnaire) is a self-report questionnaire that measures meaning in life. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: mean. Two INDEPENDENT subscale means: Presence of Meaning (5 items) and Search for Meaning (5 items). Do NOT sum into a total. Validation (Steger et al. 2006): alphas Presence = .88, Search = .93. Audit finding (consolidate.md): Rilev's 'Meaning Gap' outcome is a Rilev-derived transformation (typically Presence minus Search or similar) — NOT a published MLQ dimension. No clinical cutoffs.

Subscales

  • Presence of Meaning — items 1, 4, 5, 6, 9
  • Search for Meaning — items 2, 3, 7, 8, 10

What the scores mean

There is no official cutoff — this instrument produces a continuous score that is interpreted relative to comparison groups rather than a pass/fail threshold.

Usage & licensing

Free — Available for research use with attribution (Steger et al. 2006); no fee.
