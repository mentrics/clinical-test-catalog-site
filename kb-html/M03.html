CES-D — plain-language guide

What it measures: Depression symptoms (Mood)

Who it's for: Adolescents/adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 20 items · **Scores from 0 to 60

What this test is

CES-D (Center for Epidemiologic Studies Depression Scale) is a self-report questionnaire that measures depression symptoms. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

What the questions look like

Each item is answered on one scale: Rarely or none of the time (less than 1 day) (0), Some or a little of the time (1-2 days) (1), Occasionally or a moderate amount of the time (3-4 days) (2), Most or all of the time (5-7 days) (3).

Example items (20 total) - I was bothered by things that usually don't bother me. - I did not feel like eating; my appetite was poor. - I felt that I could not shake off the blues even with help from my family or friends. - I felt that I was just as good as other people. - I had trouble keeping my mind on what I was doing. - … (15 more — see the full item list on the detail page)

How it's scored

Method: sum. Sum all 20 items; items 4, 8, 12, 16 are reverse-scored (positive items: 0->3, 1->2, 2->1, 3->0). Original validation (Radloff 1977): alpha = .84-.90 across general-population samples. Conventional clinical cutoff >=16 (Radloff 1977); >=23 for major depression-level severity in some settings. Screening, not diagnostic.

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–15 No significant depressive symptoms Below conventional screening threshold
16–26 Elevated — possible depression At/above conventional cutoff (16); follow-up clinical interview recommended
27–60 Severe depressive symptomatology High symptom burden; clinical assessment strongly indicated

Usage & licensing

Free — Widely reproduced for research; developed with NIMH funding. No fee for research/clinical use.
