Stanford Sleepiness Scale — plain-language guide

What it measures: Sleepiness (Sleep)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 1 items · **Scores from 1 to 7

What this test is

Stanford Sleepiness Scale (Stanford Sleepiness Scale) is a self-report questionnaire that measures sleepiness. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: sum. SSS: a SINGLE-ITEM state sleepiness rating (not a multi-item scale). Score = the selected descriptor level 1-7. Validation (Hoddes et al. 1973): sensitive to sleep deprivation and circadian variation; used for repeated within-day administration. Higher = sleepier at that moment. No clinical cutoffs — momentary state measure.

What the scores mean

There is no official cutoff — this instrument produces a continuous score that is interpreted relative to comparison groups rather than a pass/fail threshold.

Usage & licensing

Free — Freely available for research use with attribution (Hoddes, Zarcone, Smythe, Phillips & Dement 1973).

Source

Hoddes, E., Zarcone, V., Smythe, H., Phillips, R., & Dement, W. C. (1973). Quantification of sleepiness: A new approach. Psychophysiology, 10(4), 431-436. · DOI
