DSS-B — plain-language guide

What it measures: Dissociation (Trauma)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 8 items · **Scores from 0 to 24

What this test is

DSS-B (Brief Dissociative Symptoms Scale) is a self-report questionnaire that measures dissociation. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

What the questions look like

Each item is answered on one scale: Not at all (0), A little bit (1), Quite a bit (2), A lot (3).

Example items (8 total) - In the past 7 days, I have had moments when I felt as if I were watching myself from a distance. - In the past 7 days, I have felt as if I were 'outside' of my body. - In the past 7 days, I have had moments when things that were happening around me seemed unreal. - In the past 7 days, I have felt as if I were in a fog or a daze. - In the past 7 days, I have had moments when I could not remember something important that I needed to remember. - … (3 more — see the full item list on the detail page)

How it's scored

Method: sum. Sum all 8 items. Derived from the 20-item Dissociative Symptoms Scale (DSS); assesses 4 facets (depersonalization/derealization, disruption of consciousness/memory, dissociative amnesia, disembodied experience) with 2 items each. Validation (Blevins, Weathers & Davis 2017; also Armour et al. 2017): alpha = .85-.88 across clinical and nonclinical samples. No official diagnostic cutoff — interpret as a continuous dissociation-severity signal. DSS-B: no official diagnostic cutoff — continuous dissociation measure (Blevins et al. 2017). Informal research heuristic bands added for runner interpretation only.

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–7 Low dissociation Below research heuristic elevated range
8–15 Elevated dissociation At/above informal research elevation threshold
16–24 High dissociation High dissociation severity signal

Usage & licensing

Public domain — US government work (VA National Center for PTSD); free to use with attribution.
