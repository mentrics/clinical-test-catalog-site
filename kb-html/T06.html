DES-II — plain-language guide

What it measures: Dissociation (Trauma)

Who it's for: Adolescents/adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 28 items · **Scores from 0 to 100

What this test is

DES-II (Dissociative Experiences Scale-II) is a self-report questionnaire that measures dissociation. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: mean. DES-II: mean of all 28 item percentages = overall DES score. 3 subscales (Amnestic dissociation, Absorption/imaginative involvement, Depersonalization/derealization) computed as mean of their items. Validation (Bernstein & Putnam 1986; Carlson & Putnam 1993 DES-II): test-retest r = .84; subscale alphas .79-.90. High DES (>=30) distinguishes dissociative-disorder populations from other clinical and nonclinical samples; 60+ is rare in nonclinical populations.

Subscales

  • Amnestic dissociation — mean
  • Absorption / imaginative involvement — mean
  • Depersonalization / derealization — mean

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–29 Below high-dissociation threshold Dissociation not prominent (high-threshold interpretation)
30–59 Elevated dissociation At/above 30 — dissociative-disorder screening threshold; careful clinical evaluation indicated
60–100 Very high dissociation Rare in nonclinical populations; dissociative-disorder evaluation strongly indicated
