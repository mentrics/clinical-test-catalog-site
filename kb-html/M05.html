MADRS-S — plain-language guide

What it measures: Depression (Mood)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 9 items · **Scores from 0 to 27

What this test is

MADRS-S (Montgomery-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale — Self-report) is a self-report questionnaire that measures depression. The instrument is established, but there are caveats worth knowing (version, scoring, or derived-score issues).

How it's scored

Method: sum. Sum all 9 items (self-report version uses 9 of the 10 clinician items, dropping 'observed sadness'). Original MADRS validation (Montgomery & Åsberg 1979): sensitivity to treatment change established. MADRS-S validation (Svanborg & Åsberg 1994): alpha = .88, correlation with clinician MADRS r = .87. Range corrected 2026-09-09: MADRS-S self-report is 9 items 0-3 = 0-27 (clinician MADRS 0-60 bands leaked in; scaled to MADRS-S range). Verify against Svanborg & Asberg manual.

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–6 Normal No clinically relevant depression (self-report range 0-27)
7–13 Mild Mild depression (MADRS-S bands, scaled)
14–20 Moderate Moderate depression
21–27 Severe Severe depression (MADRS-S range cap)

Usage & licensing

Restricted — MADRS is copyrighted (original publication widely cited; Swedish and English versions distributed via licensing partners). Self-report adaptation (MADRS-S/SV) availability varies by country.
