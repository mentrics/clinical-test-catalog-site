SVS — plain-language guide

What it measures: Subjective vitality (Positive Psychology)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 7 items · **Scores from 1 to 6

What this test is

SVS (Portrait Values Questionnaire — Short Form (Schwartz Value Survey)) is a self-report questionnaire that measures subjective vitality. The instrument is established, but there are caveats worth knowing (version, scoring, or derived-score issues).

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: mean. SVS (Schwartz Value Survey — the 21-item short form version referenced in the catalog as 'SVS'): 10 basic values arranged in the Schwartz circumplex (Self-Direction, Stimulation, Hedonism, Achievement, Power, Security, Conformity, Tradition, Benevolence, Universalism). Score = mean per value after ipsative centering (subtract the respondent's overall mean — values are compared RELATIVELY). Validation (Schwartz 2006): alphas .56-.75 (short form; modest). Catalog lists VM01 (PVQ) and PP04 (SVS) as separate instruments — they may be the same scale family; verify whether the catalog intends two distinct value measures.

What the scores mean

There is no official cutoff — this instrument produces a continuous score that is interpreted relative to comparison groups rather than a pass/fail threshold.

Usage & licensing

Free — Free for research use with attribution (Schwartz 2006); ESS Human Values Scale variant also available.

Source

Schwartz, S. H. (2006). Basic human values: Theory, measurement, and applications. Revue Française de Sociologie, 47(4), 249-282.

Scoring rules from: Schwartz (2006) value key + centering instructions

Note: Possible duplicate/overlap with VM01 (PVQ) — the catalog lists both; verify whether PP04 is a distinct SVS form or the same instrument relabeled.
