PVQ — plain-language guide

What it measures: Personal values (Values/Motivation)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 40 items · **Scores from 1 to 6

What this test is

PVQ (Portrait Values Questionnaire (short)) is a self-report questionnaire that measures personal values. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: mean. PVQ-21 (short): 21 items → 10 basic values (Self-Direction, Stimulation, Hedonism, Achievement, Power, Security, Conformity, Tradition, Benevolence, Universalism) — 2 items per value except Hedonism (3). Score = mean per value after centering (raw means OR ipsative centering — subtract each respondent's overall mean to correct for acquiescence; ipsative scoring is Schwartz's recommended approach). Validation (Schwartz 2006): alphas .56-.75 (short form; modest — values are broad constructs).

What the scores mean

There is no official cutoff — this instrument produces a continuous score that is interpreted relative to comparison groups rather than a pass/fail threshold.

Usage & licensing

Free — Free for research use with attribution (Schwartz 2006 PVQ-21; Schwartz et al. 2001 PVQ-40); ESS version also available.

Source

Schwartz, S. H. (2006). Basic human values: Theory, measurement, and applications. Revue Française de Sociologie, 47(4), 249-282. (PVQ-40: Schwartz et al. 2001, Journal of Cross-Cultural Psychology.)

Scoring rules from: Schwartz (2006) value key + centering instructions
