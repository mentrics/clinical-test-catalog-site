AQ — plain-language guide

What it measures: Autistic traits (Neurodevelopment)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 50 items · **Scores from 0 to 50

What this test is

AQ (Autism Spectrum Quotient) is a self-report questionnaire that measures autistic traits. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: sum. Half the items are keyed 'agree'=1, half 'disagree'=1 (attention-switching and imagination items reverse) — the official key matrix is required. Sum key-correct responses. Original validation (Baron-Cohen et al. 2001): test-retest r = .85; cutoff 26+ distinguished autistic adults from controls (80% sensitivity in clinical group, 2% of controls at 26+; 32+ often cited as high-sensitivity threshold). Screening instrument — NOT diagnostic.

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–25 Below screening threshold Few autistic-trait indicators
26–31 Elevated autistic traits At the original 26 cutoff; consider full assessment
32–50 High autistic traits High-sensitivity threshold (32+); formal autism assessment indicated

Usage & licensing
