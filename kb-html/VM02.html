BPNSFS — plain-language guide

What it measures: Basic psychological needs (Values/Motivation)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 24 items · **Scores from 1 to 5

What this test is

BPNSFS (Basic Psychological Need Satisfaction and Frustration Scale) is a self-report questionnaire that measures basic psychological needs. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: mean. BPNSFS: 24 items, 3 need subscales x 2 directions — Autonomy Satisfaction (4) + Autonomy Frustration (4), Competence Satisfaction (4) + Competence Frustration (4), Relatedness Satisfaction (4) + Relatedness Frustration (4). Score = mean per subscale (6 subscales); satisfaction and frustration are related but distinct (NOT simple reverses). Validation (Chen et al. 2015): alphas .79-.86 across subscales; 6-factor structure confirmed across cultures. No clinical cutoffs.

Subscales

  • Autonomy Satisfaction — mean
  • Autonomy Frustration — mean
  • Competence Satisfaction — mean
  • Competence Frustration — mean
  • Relatedness Satisfaction — mean
  • Relatedness Frustration — mean

What the scores mean

There is no official cutoff — this instrument produces a continuous score that is interpreted relative to comparison groups rather than a pass/fail threshold.

Usage & licensing

Free — Freely available for research use with attribution (Chen et al. 2015).
