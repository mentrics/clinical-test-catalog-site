BFNE — plain-language guide

What it measures: Fear of negative evaluation (Anxiety)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 12 items · **Scores from 12 to 60

What this test is

BFNE (Brief Fear of Negative Evaluation Scale) is a self-report questionnaire that measures fear of negative evaluation. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: sum. BFNE = 12 items; sum (4 reverse-keyed: items 2, 4, 7, 10 in standard numbering — official key required). Validation (Leary 1983): alpha = .90. BFNE-S (8-item straight-keyed variant, Rodebaugh et al. 2004) is psychometrically superior (less ambiguity, better fit). Cutoff: no universal official threshold; higher = greater fear of negative evaluation (core social-anxiety mechanism).

What the scores mean

There is no official cutoff — this instrument produces a continuous score that is interpreted relative to comparison groups rather than a pass/fail threshold.
