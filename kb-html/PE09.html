MMPI-3 — plain-language guide

What it measures: Personality pathology (Personality)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 335 items · **Scores from 0 to 117

What this test is

MMPI-3 (Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory-3) is a self-report questionnaire that measures personality pathology. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

How it's scored

Method: algorithm. MMPI-3 (335 items): 8 validity scales (including SIR, F, Fp, FBS, K, VRIN, TRIN, DS), 10 empirical clinical scales (Hs, D, Hy, Pd, Mf, Pa, Pt, Sc, Ma, Si), 9 Restructured Clinical (RC) scales, 28 Specific Problems (SP) scales, 5 Psychosocial/Psy-5 scales, plus higher-order scales. Interpretation via T-scores (M = 50, SD = 10) against normative samples; T >=65 = clinically significant. MMPI-3 uses updated 2015+ normative samples. Validation (Ben-Porath & Tellegen 2020 manual; Tellegen et al. 2003 for RC scales).

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–117 T-score interpretation All scales interpreted via T-scores; T >=65 is the conventional clinical significance threshold. Raw scale scores alone are meaningless without the official norm tables and code-type interpretive system.
