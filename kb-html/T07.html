ACE Questionnaire — plain-language guide

What it measures: Trauma exposure (Trauma)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 10 items · **Scores from 0 to 10

What this test is

ACE Questionnaire (Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACE) Questionnaire) is a self-report questionnaire that measures trauma exposure. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

What the questions look like

Each item is answered on one scale: Binary yes/no per category; 'yes' requires occurrence before age 18. Items map to 10 ACE categories (3 abuse, 2 neglect, 5 household challenges)..

Example items (4 total) - Was a biological parent ever lost to you through divorce, abandonment, or separation? - Did you often feel that your family didn't look out for each other, feel close to each other, or support each other? - Did you often experience a lack of food, clean clothes, or someone to protect you? - Were your parents ever separated or divorced? (Note: variant orderings exist across official versions; confirm the exact form used.)

How it's scored

Method: sum. ACE score = number of categories experienced (not number of incidents). Landmark dose-response findings (Felitti et al. 1998; CDC 2010s data): ACE 4+ associated with substantially elevated risk of depression, substance use, and chronic disease; ACE 6+ = high-risk tier. The ACE score is a RISK indicator, not a diagnostic or severity-of-symptoms measure.

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–0 ACE 0 No categories endorsed
1–3 ACE 1-3 Elevated risk relative to 0; dose-response gradient applies
4–5 ACE 4-5 High risk — odds ratios for mental-health and substance-use outcomes are 2-4x vs ACE 0 (Felitti 1998)
6–10 ACE 6+ Very high risk tier; trauma-informed care strongly indicated

Usage & licensing

Free — Public health surveillance instrument (CDC-Kaiser); freely usable with attribution. Multiple variants exist (original 10-category, CDC BRFSS version, international adaptations).

Source

Felitti, V. J., Anda, R. F., Nordenberg, D., et al. (1998). Relationship of childhood abuse and household dysfunction to many of the leading causes of death in adults. American Journal of Preventive Medicine, 14(4), 245-258. · DOI

Scoring rules from: Felitti et al. (1998); CDC BRFSS ACE module scoring guidance
