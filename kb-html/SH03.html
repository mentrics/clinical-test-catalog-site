BSS — plain-language guide

What it measures: Suicidal ideation (Suicide/Self-harm)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 21 items · **Scores from 0 to 38

What this test is

BSS (Beck Scale for Suicide Ideation) is a self-report questionnaire that measures suicidal ideation. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: algorithm. BSS (21 items): 19 items scored only if items 4 (wish to die) and 9 (desire to make active suicide attempt) are both positive (screening items); otherwise total = 0. Total = sum of 19 scored items (0-38). Validation (Beck, Steer & Ranieri 1988 BSI psychometrics): alpha = .90; discriminates attempters from non-attempters. Cutoffs (Beck et al. 1988): total >=6 clinically-relevant suicide ideation (sensitivity .92, specificity .84 in psychiatric samples).

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–5 Below threshold Suicide ideation not clinically significant
6–38 Clinically relevant suicide ideation 6+ — risk assessment indicated
