DERS — plain-language guide

What it measures: Difficulties in regulation (Emotion Regulation)

Who it's for: Adolescents/adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 36 items · **Scores from 1 to 5

What this test is

DERS (Difficulties in Emotion Regulation Scale) is a self-report questionnaire that measures difficulties in regulation. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: mean. DERS (36 items): 6 subscales — Nonacceptance (6 items), Goals (5), Impulse (6), Awareness (6), Strategies (5), Clarity (5) + total (mean of all 36 after reverse-keying Awareness items 4, 18, 22, 29, 34 and Clarity items 1, 6, 10, 16 per official key). Validation (Gratz & Roemer 2004): alphas .80-.89 across subscales. Higher = greater dysregulation. No clinical cutoffs.

Subscales

  • Nonacceptance of emotional responses — mean
  • Difficulties engaging in goal-directed behavior — mean
  • Impulse control difficulties — mean
  • Lack of emotional awareness — mean
  • Limited access to emotion regulation strategies — mean
  • Lack of emotional clarity — mean

What the scores mean

There is no official cutoff — this instrument produces a continuous score that is interpreted relative to comparison groups rather than a pass/fail threshold.

Usage & licensing

Free — Freely available for research/clinical use with attribution (Gratz & Roemer 2004).

Source

Gratz, K. L., & Roemer, L. (2004). Multidimensional assessment of emotion regulation and dysregulation. Journal of Psychopathology and Behavioral Assessment, 26(1), 41-54. · DOI
