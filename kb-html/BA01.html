PGSI — plain-language guide

What it measures: Gambling (Behavioral Addiction)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 9 items · **Scores from 0 to 27

What this test is

PGSI (Problem Gambling Severity Index) is a self-report questionnaire that measures gambling. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: sum. Sum all 9 items. Validation (Ferris & Wynne 2001): alpha = .92-.94; cutoffs from ROC analysis in the original Canadian validation (revised in Currie et al. 2004 to 4.58 for non-problem). Standard PGSI bands (Ferris & Wynne 2001): 0 = non-problem, 1-2 low risk, 3-7 moderate risk, 8+ problem gambling.

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–0 Non-problem gambling No gambling-related problem indicators
1–2 Low-risk gambling Few indicators; monitor
3–7 Moderate-risk gambling Moderate problem indicators; brief intervention indicated
8–27 Problem gambling 8+ — probable problem gambling; clinical assessment indicated

Usage & licensing

Free — Freely available for clinical/research use (Ferris & Wynne 2001, Canadian Centre on Substance Abuse).

Source

Ferris, J., & Wynne, H. (2001). The Canadian Problem Gambling Index: Final report. Canadian Centre on Substance Abuse. Psychometric refinement: Currie, S. R., Hodgins, D. C., & Casey, D. M. (2013). International Gambling Studies.

Scoring rules from: Ferris & Wynne (2001), bands + psychometrics
