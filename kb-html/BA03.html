IAT — plain-language guide

What it measures: Problematic internet use (Behavioral Addiction)

Who it's for: Adolescents/adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 20 items · **Scores from 20 to 100

What this test is

IAT (Internet Addiction Test) is a self-report questionnaire that measures problematic internet use. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: sum. IAT: 20 items, single total (some research proposes 6-factor or 3-factor structures but scoring convention is the total). Validation (Young 1998; Widyanto & McMurran 2004 psychometric review): alpha = .93. Cutoffs (Young 1998 heuristic): 20-49 normal internet use, 50-79 occasional/frequent problems, 80-100 significant problematic use. Young's bands are heuristic, not diagnostically validated.

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
20–49 Normal internet use (heuristic) Below problematic-use threshold (Young 1998 heuristic bands, not validated diagnostically)
50–79 Occasional/frequent problems (heuristic) Problematic internet-use indicators; monitor
80–100 Significant problematic use (heuristic) Significant problematic use; clinical assessment indicated

Usage & licensing

Free — Widely available for research use with attribution (Young 1998).
