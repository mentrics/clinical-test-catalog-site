MDQ — plain-language guide

What it measures: Bipolar screening (Psychosis/Bipolar)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 15 items · **Scores from 0 to 3

What this test is

MDQ (Mood Disorder Questionnaire) is a self-report questionnaire that measures bipolar screening. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

What the questions look like

Each item is answered on one scale: Two parts: items 1-13 yes/no (0/1); item 14 = co-occurrence (yes/no); item 15 = impairment (none/minor/moderate/serious = 0-3). Symptom items refer to a single episode period, per form..

Example items (15 total) - ...there was a period of time when you felt so good or so hyper that other people thought you were not your normal self, or you were so hyper that you got into trouble? - ...there was a period of time when you were so irritable that you shouted at people or started fights or arguments? - ...you suddenly felt that you were much more self-confident than usual? - ...you got much less sleep than usual and found that you didn't really miss it? - ...you were much more talkative or spoke much faster than usual? - … (10 more — see the full item list on the detail page)

How it's scored

Method: algorithm. POSITIVE SCREEN requires ALL THREE: (1) 7+ of 13 symptom items endorsed 'yes', (2) item 14 'yes' (co-occurrence), (3) item 15 'moderate' or 'serious' impairment. Original validation (Hirschfeld et al. 2000): sensitivity 0.73 / specificity 0.90 in psychiatric outpatient samples; ~0.28/0.90 in community samples (Hirschfeld 2003) — screen positive → full clinical interview (e.g., SCID module).

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–0 Screen negative Bipolar-spectrum screening criteria not met (any of the 3 rules failed)
1–1 Screen positive All 3 criteria met (7+ symptoms, co-occurring, impairing) — evaluation indicated

Usage & licensing

Free — Freely distributed for clinical screening use (AstraZeneca-era distribution); no fee for research with attribution.

Source

Hirschfeld, R. M. A., et al. (2000). Development and validation of a screening instrument for bipolar spectrum disorder: The Mood Disorder Questionnaire. American Journal of Psychiatry, 157(11), 1873-1875. · DOI

Scoring rules from: Hirschfeld et al. (2000) scoring rules; community-sample psychometrics Hirschfeld et al. (2003), J Clin Psychiatry

This guide is for information only — it is not a substitute for professional assessment. Scores, especially on screening tools, never equal a diagnosis.
