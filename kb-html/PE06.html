PID-5 — plain-language guide

What it measures: Pathological traits (Personality)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 220 items · **Scores from 0 to 660

What this test is

PID-5 (Personality Inventory for DSM-5 — Adult) is a self-report questionnaire that measures pathological traits. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: sum. PID-5 (220 items): 5 higher-order domains x 25 facets total (15-25 items per domain). Domain scores = mean of facet means (each facet = mean of its 4 items). Maladaptive domains: Negative Affect, Detachment, Antagonism, Disinhibition, Psychoticism. Validation (Krueger, Derringer, Markon, Watson & Skodol 2012): domain alphas .90-.96. No clinical cutoffs in the standard form — interpretation against mean/distribution or clinical norms (some versions publish age/gender-corrected T-scores).

What the scores mean

There is no official cutoff — this instrument produces a continuous score that is interpreted relative to comparison groups rather than a pass/fail threshold.

Usage & licensing

Public domain — Public domain — developed by the NIDH-funded workgroup for DSM-5; free from American Psychiatric Association (psychiatry.org/PID-5).
