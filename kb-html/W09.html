RSES — plain-language guide

What it measures: Self-esteem (Well-being)

Who it's for: Adolescents/adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 10 items · **Scores from 10 to 40

What this test is

RSES (Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale) is a self-report questionnaire that measures self-esteem. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: sum. Sum all 10 items (5 reverse-keyed: items 2, 4, 6, 7, 9 — 0/1/2/3 scoring after reversing, giving 0-30, or 1/2/3/4 giving 10-40; both conventions published). Validation (Rosenberg 1965; Gray-Little et al. 2009 update): alpha = .77-.88; RSES is the most widely used self-esteem measure. Interpretation: 0-15 low, 15-25 normal, 25-30 high (informal bands, not officially standardized). Bands corrected 2026-09-09 to the actual 10-40 range (1-4 convention).

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
10–24 Low self-esteem (informal) Below typical range (heuristic)
25–34 Normal self-esteem (informal) Typical range (heuristic)
35–40 High self-esteem (informal) Above typical range (heuristic)
