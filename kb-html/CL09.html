PROMIS Global Health — plain-language guide

What it measures: General psychopathology (Clinical)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 10 items · **Scores from 0 to 100

What this test is

PROMIS Global Health (PROMIS Global Health) is a self-report questionnaire that measures general psychopathology. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: algorithm. PROMIS Global Health (10 items): produces 2 summary scores — Global Physical Health (GPH, 3 items) and Global Mental Health (GMH, 4 items), plus a Global Health total. Scores are IRT-transformed to T-metrics (M = 50, SD = 10, US general population), often rescaled 0-100. Validation (Hays et al. 2010): alphas GPH .81, GMH .86. Cutoffs: T 50 = population mean; T >=60 commonly flagged as clinically elevated.

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–59 Within normal range (T<60) Global health at/below 1 SD above the population mean (lower T = better for the negative-oriented scoring, depending on direction — verify scoring direction per healthmeasures.net)
60–69 Elevated (T 60-69) 1-2 SD above mean — clinically notable global health burden
70–100 Very elevated (T>=70) 2+ SD above mean — severe global health burden
