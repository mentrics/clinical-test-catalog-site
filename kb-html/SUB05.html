What this test is

TAPS (Tobacco, Alcohol, Prescription Medication, and other Substance Use tool) is a self-report questionnaire that measures substance screening. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: algorithm. TAPS has two parts: TAPS-1 (combined screen) and TAPS-2 (prescription/other-drug follow-up). Scoring: tobacco and alcohol classified by frequency band (none / low / moderate / high risk per NIAAA/NIDA guidelines); prescription and illicit drugs use a 2-stage design — any 'yes' on TAPS-1 triggers TAPS-2 follow-up, and TAPS-2 classifies by past-3-month use + problem indicators. Each substance category gets its own risk level; there is NO single total. Validation (McNeely et al. 2016): AUROC .81-.97 across substances vs. reference interview.

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–5 Per-substance risk classification Each substance category independently classified as none/low/moderate/high risk per NIDA TAPS-2 scoring guide (tobacco/alcohol frequency bands; prescription/other drugs by TAPS-1 trigger + TAPS-2 problem count).

Usage & licensing

Public domain — NIDA CTN-developed; public-domain US government work (McNeely, Subramaniam, et al. 2016).
