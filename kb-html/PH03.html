PCS — plain-language guide

What it measures: Pain catastrophizing (Physical Health)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 13 items · **Scores from 0 to 52

What this test is

PCS (Pain Catastrophizing Scale) is a self-report questionnaire that measures pain catastrophizing. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: sum. PCS: 13 items, 3 subscales — Rumination (4 items), Magnification (3), Helplessness (6). Subscale sums + total (higher = greater catastrophizing). Validation (Sullivan et al. 1995): alpha = .87 total; subscales .76-.86. Interpretation: no official clinical cutoffs, but PCS total >=30 commonly treated as high catastrophizing in pain research (Sullivan's published guidance).

Subscales

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–29 Below high-catastrophizing threshold Below the commonly applied 30 threshold
30–52 High catastrophizing 30+ — high catastrophizing; cognitive-behavioral pain intervention relevant
