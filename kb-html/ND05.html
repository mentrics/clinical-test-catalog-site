WURS — plain-language guide

What it measures: Adult ADHD symptoms (Neurodevelopment)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 25 items · **Scores from 0 to 100

What this test is

WURS (Wender Utah Rating Scale) is a self-report questionnaire that measures adult adhd symptoms. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: sum. WURS (61 items): 25 scored items + 36 filler items (to disguise purpose). Score = sum of the 25 scored items (0-100). Validation (Ward et al. 1993): alpha = .94; discriminates ADHD adults from controls and from depressed/mood-disorder adults retrospectively. Cutoffs (Ward et al. 1993): total >=46 suggests childhood ADHD (sensitivity .86, specificity .99 vs normal controls; lower specificity vs psychiatric controls).

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–45 Below threshold Childhood ADHD indicators not prominent
46–100 Positive screen 46+ — possible childhood ADHD; retrospective recall limits reliability — corroborate with records/informants
