MFQ — plain-language guide

What it measures: Adolescent depression (Child/Adolescent)

Who it's for: Children/adolescents · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 33 items · **Scores from 0 to 52

What this test is

MFQ (Mood and Feelings Questionnaire) is a self-report questionnaire that measures adolescent depression. The instrument is established, but there are caveats worth knowing (version, scoring, or derived-score issues).

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: sum. MFQ (full 33-item version): sum of all 33 items (higher = more depressive symptoms). Short forms: MFQ-S (11 items, 0-22) and MFQ-C/D variants. Validation (Costello & Angold 1988; Burleson Daviss et al. 2006 psychometrics): alphas .85-.92. Cutoffs (Burleson Daviss et al. 2006 for MFQ-S): >=12 suggests major depressive disorder (sensitivity .83, specificity .77); full-MFQ cutoffs vary by study (~27+ common).

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–26 Below high-symptom threshold Below common full-MFQ clinical concern threshold (~27+; verify against the specific MFQ variant used)
27–52 Elevated depressive symptoms At/above common threshold — clinical assessment indicated
