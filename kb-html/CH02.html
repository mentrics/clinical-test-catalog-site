YSR — plain-language guide

What it measures: Broad psychopathology (Child/Adolescent)

Who it's for: Adolescents · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 112 items · **Scores from 0 to 226

What this test is

YSR (Youth Self-Report (11-18)) is a self-report questionnaire that measures broad psychopathology. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: algorithm. YSR (112 scored items): 8 syndrome scales (same structure as CBCL/6-18) + Internalizing/Externalizing broadband factors + Total Problems. Scale scores = raw sums converted to T-scores against gender/age normative samples; T >=65 = borderline, T >=70 = clinically significant. Validation (Achenbach & Rescorla 2001): parallel to CBCL psychometrics.

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–226 T-score interpretation Same ASEBA thresholds as CBCL: T 60-63 borderline, T 64-69 borderline clinical, T 70+ clinically significant. Official ASEBA norm tables (gender/age) required for conversion.
