ERQ-S — plain-language guide

What it measures: Emotion regulation short form (Emotion Regulation)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 6 items · **Scores from 1 to 7

What this test is

ERQ-S (Emotion Regulation Questionnaire — Short Form (ERQ-S)) is a self-report questionnaire that measures emotion regulation short form. The instrument is established, but there are caveats worth knowing (version, scoring, or derived-score issues).

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: mean. ERQ-S (Preece, Becerra, Hasking et al. 2020): 6 items, 2 subscales — Reappraisal (3 items) and Suppression (3 items). Score = mean per subscale. Validation (Preece et al. 2020): alphas .75-.79 (comparable to full ERQ). Audit finding (consolidate.md): Rilev uses 'ERQ-S' — the AGENTS.md version-ambiguity list flags this. Confirm whether Rilev means the Preece et al. (2020) 6-item form or a different short form (e.g., 6-item Italian ERQ-S). No clinical cutoffs.

Subscales

  • Reappraisal — mean
  • Suppression — mean

What the scores mean

There is no official cutoff — this instrument produces a continuous score that is interpreted relative to comparison groups rather than a pass/fail threshold.

Usage & licensing

Free — Freely available for research use with attribution; ERQ-S is a 6-item short form of the 10-item ERQ (Preece et al. 2020).

Source

Preece, D. A., Becerra, R., Robinson, K., et al. (2020). The Emotion Regulation Questionnaire at 18 years: A systematic review and meta-analysis of psychometric properties. (ERQ-S short form: Preece et al. 2020, Frontiers in Psychology / PLOS ONE.)
