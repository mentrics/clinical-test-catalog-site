PROMIS Fatigue — plain-language guide

What it measures: Fatigue (Physical Health)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: Variable items · **Scores from 0 to 100

What this test is

PROMIS Fatigue (PROMIS Fatigue (Short Form)) is a self-report questionnaire that measures fatigue. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

How it's scored

Method: algorithm. PROMIS Fatigue uses IRT-based T-score scoring (item responses mapped to theta via healthmeasures.net lookup tables), standardized to T-metric (M = 50, SD = 10, US general population). Higher T = more fatigue. Multiple short forms (4a, 6a, 7a, 8a, 10a, 13a) exist — confirm which form the catalog carries. Validation (Cella et al. 2007; Lai et al. 2011 for fatigue bank): alpha ~.95+. Cutoffs: T 50 = population mean; T >=60 (1 SD above) commonly flagged as clinically elevated.

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–59 Within normal range (T<60) Fatigue at/below 1 SD above the population mean
60–69 Elevated (T 60-69) 1-2 SD above mean — clinically notable fatigue
70–100 Very elevated (T>=70) 2+ SD above mean — severe fatigue
