WAI — plain-language guide

What it measures: Work ability (Work)

Who it's for: Workers · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 7 items · **Scores from 7 to 49

What this test is

WAI (Work Ability Index) is a self-report questionnaire that measures work ability. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: sum. WAI: sum of 7 items (current work ability vs lifetime best 0-10, work-ability vs demands of job 2-10, diagnosed illness count 1-7, estimated work impairment 1-6, sick leave 1-5, prognosis 1/4/7, mental resources 1-4). Validation (Tuomi et al. 1998): WAI predicts work disability longitudinally. Cutoffs (Tuomi et al. 1998): 7-27 poor, 28-36 moderate, 37-43 good, 44-49 excellent work ability.

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
7–27 Poor work ability Poor — occupational-health intervention indicated
28–36 Moderate work ability Moderate; monitor
37–43 Good work ability Good
44–49 Excellent work ability Excellent

Usage & licensing

Free — Freely available for research/occupational-health use (Tuomi et al. 1998, Finnish Institute of Occupational Health).

Source

Tuomi, K., Ilmarinen, J., Jahkola, A., Katajarinne, L., & Tulkki, A. (1998). Work Ability Index (2nd ed.). Finnish Institute of Occupational Health.
