SCOFF — plain-language guide

What it measures: Eating disorder screen (Eating)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 5 items · **Scores from 0 to 5

What this test is

SCOFF (SCOFF questionnaire) is a self-report questionnaire that measures eating disorder screen. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

What the questions look like

Each item is answered on one scale: No (0), Yes (1).

Example items (5 total) - Do you make yourself Sick (vomit) because you feel uncomfortably full? - Do you worry you have lost Control over how much you eat? - Have you recently lost more than One stone (6.35 kg) in a three-month period? - Do you believe yourself to be Fat when others say you are too thin? - Would you say that Food dominates your life?

How it's scored

Method: sum. Sum 'yes' answers (1 each). Original validation (Morgan et al. 1999): cutoff >=2 optimizes sensitivity (85%) / specificity (88%) for eating-disorder case detection in general populations.

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–1 Negative screen Unlikely eating disorder
2–5 Positive screen Possible eating disorder; clinical interview indicated

Usage & licensing

Free — Published in BMJ (1999) by Morgan et al. with explicit intent for wide clinical use; freely reproduced for clinical/research purposes with attribution.

Source

Morgan, J. F., Reid, F., & Lacey, J. H. (1999). The SCOFF questionnaire: Assessment of a new screening tool for eating disorders. BMJ, 319(7223), 1467-1468. · DOI
