SWLS — plain-language guide

What it measures: Life satisfaction (Well-being)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 5 items · **Scores from 5 to 35

What this test is

SWLS (Satisfaction with Life Scale) is a self-report questionnaire that measures life satisfaction. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

What the questions look like

Each item is answered on one scale: Strongly disagree (1), Disagree (2), Slightly disagree (3), Neither agree nor disagree (4), Slightly agree (5), Agree (6), Strongly agree (7).

Example items (5 total) - In most ways my life is close to my ideal. - The conditions of my life are excellent. - I am satisfied with my life. - So far I have gotten the important things I want in life. - If I could live my life over, I would change almost nothing.

How it's scored

Method: sum. Sum all 5 items. Original validation (Diener 1985): alpha = .87, test-retest (2 months) r = .82. Cutoff bands from Pavot & Diener (1993) review.

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
5–9 Extremely dissatisfied Extremely dissatisfied with life
10–14 Dissatisfied Dissatisfied with life
15–19 Slightly dissatisfied Slightly below average satisfaction
20–25 Neutral Neutral — about equally satisfied and dissatisfied
26–30 Slightly satisfied Above average satisfaction
31–35 Highly satisfied Very satisfied with life
