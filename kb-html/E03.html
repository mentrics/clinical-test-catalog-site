CAARS-S — plain-language guide

What it measures: Adult ADHD (Attention/Executive)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 66 items · **Scores from 0 to 108

What this test is

CAARS-S (Conners' Adult ADHD Rating Scale — Self-Report (Screening version)) is a self-report questionnaire that measures adult adhd. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: sum. CAARS-S:S (screening version): 30 items → 4 subscales: Inattention/Memory problems, Hyperactivity/Restlessness, Impulsivity/Emotional lability, Problems with self-concept + ADHD Index (12 items discriminating ADHD from non-ADHD). T-scores (norm-referenced by gender/age) are the primary output, not raw sums. Validation (Conners, Erhardt & Sparrow 1999): alphas .80-.89 across subscales. T >=65 = clinically elevated per MHS norms.

Subscales

  • Inattention/Memory problems — sum → T-score
  • Hyperactivity/Restlessness — sum → T-score
  • Impulsivity/Emotional lability — sum → T-score
  • Problems with self-concept — sum → T-score
  • ADHD Index — sum → T-score

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–108 T-score interpretation Subscales interpreted via T-scores (M = 50, SD = 10) against gender/age norms: T 60-64 high average, T 65-69 mildly atypical, T 70+ clinically elevated. Raw sums alone are insufficient — use the official norm tables.
