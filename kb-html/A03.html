STAI — plain-language guide

What it measures: State/trait anxiety (Anxiety)

Who it's for: Adolescents/adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 40 items · **Scores from 20 to 80

What this test is

STAI (State-Trait Anxiety Inventory) is a self-report questionnaire that measures state/trait anxiety. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: sum. Two 20-item scales scored separately: State Anxiety (current) and Trait Anxiety (usual). Sum each scale 20-80 (roughly half the items reverse-keyed — official key required). Validation (Spielberger et al. 1983 manual): alphas .86-.95 across samples. No universal cutoffs — interpret against norm tables (Spielberger manual provides gender/age norms). Rough heuristics: State 40+ / Trait 40+ often treated as elevated in clinical contexts.

Subscales

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
20–37 Low anxiety (norm-referenced) Below typical clinical concern (heuristic; consult norm tables for precise percentiles)
38–44 Average anxiety (norm-referenced) Normative middle range
45–80 Elevated anxiety (norm-referenced) Above typical concern threshold (heuristic); use official norms for percentile interpretation
