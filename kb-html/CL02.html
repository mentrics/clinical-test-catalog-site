K6 — plain-language guide

What it measures: Psychological distress (Clinical)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 6 items · **Scores from 6 to 30

What this test is

K6 (Kessler Psychological Distress Scale-6) is a self-report questionnaire that measures psychological distress. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: sum. Sum all 6 items (the 6 items from K10 that best discriminate serious mental illness). Validation (Kessler et al. 2002): alpha = .89; AUROC for serious mental illness ~.90 in NHIS calibration. Cutoffs (Kessler et al. 2002; Furukawa et al. 2003): 6-12 low, 13-18 moderate, 19+ high (19+ corresponds to ~3x risk of serious mental illness in community samples).

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
6–12 Low distress Below clinically-relevant threshold
13–18 Moderate distress Moderate; monitor
19–30 High distress 19+ — substantially elevated risk of serious mental illness; assessment indicated

Usage & licensing

Free — Free for clinical/research use (Kessler et al. 2002).
