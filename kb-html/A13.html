Intolerance of Uncertainty Scale — plain-language guide

What it measures: Fear of uncertainty (Anxiety)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 27 items · **Scores from 27 to 135

What this test is

Intolerance of Uncertainty Scale (Intolerance of Uncertainty Scale (27-item original)) is a self-report questionnaire that measures fear of uncertainty. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: sum. IUS-27 (original Freeston et al. 1994): sum of all 27 items; later research (Carleton et al. 2007) identified 2 factors (inhibitory + prospective anxiety) — see IUS-12 (A10) for the short form. Validation (Freeston et al. 1994): alpha = .91; distinguishes worry-prone from non-worriers. No clinical cutoffs — continuous measure of uncertainty intolerance.

What the scores mean

There is no official cutoff — this instrument produces a continuous score that is interpreted relative to comparison groups rather than a pass/fail threshold.

Usage & licensing

Free — Freely available for research use with attribution (Freeston, Rhéaume, Letarte, Dugas & Ladouceur 1994).

Source

Freeston, M. H., Rhéaume, J., Letarte, H., Dugas, M. J., & Ladouceur, R. (1994). Why do people worry? Personality and Individual Differences, 17(6), 791-802. · DOI
