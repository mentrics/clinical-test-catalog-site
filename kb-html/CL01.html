K10 — plain-language guide

What it measures: Psychological distress (Clinical)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 10 items · **Scores from 10 to 50

What this test is

K10 (Kessler Psychological Distress Scale-10) is a self-report questionnaire that measures psychological distress. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: sum. Sum all 10 items. Validation (Kessler et al. 2002): alpha = .88-.90; discriminates community mental-health cases in national survey data. Cutoffs (Kessler et al. 2002; Andrews & Slade 2001): 10-19 low distress, 20-24 moderate, 25-30 high, 31+ very high (Australian norm bands; other countries use slightly different cutpoints). K6 (CL02) is the 6-item short form.

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
10–19 Low distress Below clinically-relevant threshold
20–24 Moderate distress Moderate psychological distress
25–30 High distress High distress; assessment indicated
31–50 Very high distress Very high; urgent clinical evaluation indicated

Usage & licensing

Free — Free for clinical/research use (Kessler et al. 2002; public-domain instrument used in national surveys).

Source

Kessler, R. C., Andrews, G., Colpe, L. J., et al. (2002). Short screening scales to monitor population prevalences and trends in non-specific psychological distress. Psychological Medicine, 32(6), 959-976. · DOI
