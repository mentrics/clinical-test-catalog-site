PANAS — plain-language guide

What it measures: Positive affect (Mood)

Who it's for: Adolescents/adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 20 items · **Scores from 10 to 50

What this test is

PANAS (Positive and Negative Affect Schedule) is a self-report questionnaire that measures positive affect. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

What the questions look like

Each item is answered on one scale: Very slightly or not at all (1), A little (2), Moderately (3), Quite a bit (4), Extremely (5).

Example items (20 total) - Interested - Distressed - Excited - Upset - Strong - … (15 more — see the full item list on the detail page)

How it's scored

Method: sum. Two independent sums: PA (10 positive-emotion words) and NA (10 negative-emotion words), each 10-50 (or means 1-5). Do NOT create a total. Original validation (Watson, Clark & Tellegen 1988): alphas .86-.90 (PA) and .84-.87 (NA). No clinical cutoffs — interpret relative to sample norms; PA/NA correlate with extraversion/neuroticism respectively. PANAS: no official cutoffs (norm-referenced research measure). For runner purposes, sample-relative bands provided from published college norms (US mean PA~3.0-3.5, NA~1.8-2.2 on 1-5 scale, summed 10-50): PA 10-29 low, 30-40 typical, 41-50 high (per subscale). Since PANAS has no cutoffs, use subscale means with heuristic bands. RUNNER NOTE: PANAS reports PA and NA as SEPARATE sums (each 10-50). The runner totals all 20 items for a single band lookup as a demonstration; consult subscale scores in scoring_rules for the published interpretation.

Subscales

  • Positive Affect — items 1, 3, 5, 9, 10, 12, 14, 16, 17, 19
  • Negative Affect — items 2, 4, 6, 7, 8, 11, 13, 15, 18, 20

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means

41–50 High (informal, per subscale) Above typical range
