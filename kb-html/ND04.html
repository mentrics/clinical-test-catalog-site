SRS-2 — plain-language guide

What it measures: Social communication (Neurodevelopment)

Who it's for: Children/adults · How it's given: Informant/self-report

Length: 65 items · **Scores from 0 to 5

What this test is

SRS-2 (Social Responsiveness Scale-2) is a informant/self-report questionnaire that measures social communication. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: algorithm. SRS-2 (65 items): 5 treatment-subscale scores (Social Awareness, Social Cognition, Social Communication, Social Motivation, Restricted Interests & Repetitive Behavior) + total score. Subscale + total raw sums converted to T-scores against gender/age/rater norms (M = 50, SD = 10). Cutoffs (Constantino & Gruber 2012): T <=59 normal, 60-65 mild, 66-75 moderate, 76+ severe social-deficit range. Validation (Constantino et al. 2003 SRS original; Brunner et al. 2018 psychometric update).

Subscales

  • Social Awareness — sum → T-score
  • Social Cognition — sum → T-score
  • Social Communication — sum → T-score
  • Social Motivation — sum → T-score
  • Restricted Interests & Repetitive Behavior — sum → T-score

What the scores mean

Score range Band What it means
0–5 T-score interpretation Total + 5 subscales as T-scores: T <=59 normal, 60-65 mild, 66-75 moderate, 76+ severe social-deficit range. Official SRS-2 norm tables (gender/age/rater) required for conversion.
