SF-36 — plain-language guide

What it measures: Health-related quality of life (Quality of Life)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 36 items · **Scores from 0 to 100

What this test is

SF-36 (SF-36 Health Survey) is a self-report questionnaire that measures health-related quality of life. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: algorithm. SF-36: 36 items → 8 scales (Physical Functioning, Role-Physical, Bodily Pain, General Health, Vitality, Social Functioning, Role-Emotional, Mental Health), each scored 0-100 (higher = better health/functioning) after item transformation per official scoring key. Two summary components: PCS (Physical Component Summary) and MCS (Mental Component Summary) via norm-based T-score aggregation. Validation (Ware & Sherbourne 1992): alphas .78-.93 across scales. No clinical cutoffs — normative T-score interpretation (M=50, SD=10 for PCS/MCS).

What the scores mean

There is no official cutoff — this instrument produces a continuous score that is interpreted relative to comparison groups rather than a pass/fail threshold.
