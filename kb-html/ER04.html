DERS-16 — plain-language guide

What it measures: Difficulties in regulation short form (Emotion Regulation)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 16 items · **Scores from 1 to 5

What this test is

DERS-16 (Difficulties in Emotion Regulation Scale-16) is a self-report questionnaire that measures difficulties in regulation short form. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: mean. DERS-16 (Bjureberg et al. 2016, Brief Version of DERS): mean of 16 items (single factor). Validation: alpha = .92 in psychiatric sample; comparable to original 36-item DERS. No clinical cutoffs — continuous measure; higher = greater dysregulation.

Subscales

  • Total (single factor) — mean

What the scores mean

There is no official cutoff — this instrument produces a continuous score that is interpreted relative to comparison groups rather than a pass/fail threshold.

Usage & licensing

Free — Free for research/clinical use with attribution (Bjureberg et al. 2016).
