RQ — plain-language guide

What it measures: Attachment prototypes (Relationships)

Who it's for: Adults · How it's given: Self-report

Length: 4 items · **Scores from 1 to 4

What this test is

RQ (Relationship Questionnaire) is a self-report questionnaire that measures attachment prototypes. This instrument is well validated in the research literature.

Item text

The exact question wording is not reproduced here (licensing/copyright). The scoring logic below is still fully documented.

How it's scored

Method: algorithm. RQ (Bartholomew & Horowitz 1991) has two scoring conventions: (1) CATEGORICAL — respondent chooses which of 4 prototypes (Secure, Dismissive/Avoidant, Preoccupied/Anxious, Fearful) best describes them; (2) DIMENSIONAL — the 4 agreement ratings (1-7 each) are treated as 4 separate prototype scores (NOT summed, NOT a 2-factor ECR-style grid). Audit note (consolidate.md): Rilev's 4 outcomes (secure/anxious/avoidant/fearful) imply the categorical or 4-score dimensional approach — scoring must be documented as categorical/prototypical, NOT as continuous attachment dimensions like ECR. No clinical cutoffs.

What the scores mean

There is no official cutoff — this instrument produces a continuous score that is interpreted relative to comparison groups rather than a pass/fail threshold.

Usage & licensing

Free — Freely available for research use with attribution (Bartholomew & Horowitz 1991).
